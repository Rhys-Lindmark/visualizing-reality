# Qin/Han insight 03 audit — logistics as an ecology

## Claim

Qin and Han imperial reach depended on a maintained loop linking productive landscapes, records, stores, transport corridors, and recurrent labor. No single canal, road, granary, wall, or transmitted workforce total measures that system.

## Source map

- **Brian Lander (2023):** peer-reviewed political-ecology synthesis for Zhengguo, Dujiangyan, Ling Canal context, granary distribution, routine maintenance, animal power, and the retrospective link between concentrated labor and Qin's political failure.
- **Chun Fung Tong (2026):** open peer-reviewed study for the Qianling ration-sufficiency directive, assigned transfers, documented shortages, the excavated Western Han Capital Granary estimate, and explicit limits on reconstructing Qin central storage and army supply.
- **Maxim Korolkov (2024), water management:** open peer-reviewed documentary study for the 41-entry late-Qin route record and for the finding that early imperial hydraulic maintenance relied heavily on local communities rather than direct central execution.
- **Maxim Korolkov (2024), southern East Asia:** peer-reviewed regional synthesis for the 214 BCE Ling Canal campaign context and the connection of the Yangzi and Pearl drainage systems.
- **Alice Yao and Wengcheong Lam (2024):** archaeological synthesis used to frame Guanzhong granaries and irrigation systems as spatial infrastructures that made fields and subjects legible.

## Visual model

The visual contains eight unlike evidence windows. A selected row exposes its input → interface → reach chain, observed anchor, accepted measure if any, interpretation, source keys, and limit. A common 360 BCE–50 CE axis locates the windows but does not interpolate between them or encode magnitude. Bar width indicates only the row's date window.

## Exclusions

- no annual grain flow, shipment tonnage, throughput, loss rate, irrigated area, yield, or worker series;
- no modern route geometry or universal Qin road map;
- no conversion of the Western Han Capital Granary into a Qin capacity or soldier-month estimate;
- no acceptance of the transmitted 700,000-worker palace-and-mausoleum figure as a measured workforce;
- no wall length, labor share, mortality, revolt-causation, logistics-efficiency, or state-capacity score;
- no claim that early Han maintenance rules reproduce Qin law word for word.

## Data contract

`qin-logistics-ecology.csv` contains eight rows and one immutable client snapshot. Each row preserves a bounded date, system role, place, evidence class, observed anchor, input, transformation, output, source keys, interpretation, and limit. Quantitative labels are row-specific and are never averaged or converted into a common scale.

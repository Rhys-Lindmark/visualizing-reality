# Iron Age insight 02 — fuel and experimental smelting audit

## Bounded claim

Charcoal was indispensable to bloomery smelting, but charcoal mass was not a production function. Ore chemistry, furnace geometry, airflow, charging, slag behavior, weather, preparation, and operator judgment changed whether a run produced a forgeable bloom at all. The visual therefore compares published modern experiments as analogs and never converts them into ancient output, woodland loss, or army supply.

## Evidence used

- Humphris et al. (2018) publish measurable inputs and outcomes for full-scale experiments in a replica Meroitic furnace. MS1 consumed 41 kg ore and 105 kg charcoal for a 4.325 kg bloom; MS2 used 30 and 100 kg with no recognizable bloom; MS3 used 30 and 86.5 kg for 1.5 kg; MS4 used 34 and 71.25 kg with no bloom.
- Helmreich, Kobbe, and Sauerwein (2025) report ore, charcoal, and total bloomery-iron masses for seven reusable-shaft experiments. Inputs overlap while bloom outputs range from 0.02 to 2.4 kg.
- Stepanov et al. (2022) show why a ratio is insufficient: four successful shaft-furnace experiments used roughly 1:1 ore-charcoal charges, yet yield, carbon, impurities, and forgeability depended on airflow, charging rate, slag, ore, and skilled adjustment. Their explicit warning that the technique is not a reconstruction of one Southern Levantine ancient context is retained.
- Devereaux's bloomery synthesis is used for the qualitative production chain only. Its experimental examples are not treated as universal ancient coefficients.

## Derived fields

The interface computes charcoal per kilogram of reported bloom from each row. This is a transparent arithmetic transform of experiment-level inputs, not a historical estimate. A zero or trace bloom is displayed categorically rather than as infinity or as the scale-setting maximum.

## Prohibited inferences

- no ancient annual output, productivity, market share, or workforce estimate;
- no woodland hectares, deforestation curve, sustainable-yield claim, or universal wood-to-charcoal ratio;
- no claim that the experiments reproduce the exact ancient recipe;
- no missing-bloom-equals-no-metal assumption;
- no ranking of civilizations, furnaces, or smelters by efficiency;
- no projection from bloom mass to weapons, soldiers, or state capacity.

# Persia insight 01 — roads and relays audit

## Published claim

Achaemenid roads did not abolish imperial distance. Staffed stages, stores,
authorizations, and fresh carriers let messages move as a relay while ordinary
journeys remained long.

## Evidence classes kept separate

- **Literary itinerary:** Herodotus 5.52–54 supplies six Sardis–Susa segments,
  111 stages, 450 parasangs, and his ninety-day arithmetic. The visualization
  retains parasangs and does not invent surveyed geometry or convert them to a
  modern distance.
- **Literary relay description:** Herodotus 8.98 describes successive horses
  and riders, one for each day-stage. It gives no Sardis–Susa courier duration.
  Later seven-day claims are excluded from the plotted comparison.
- **Administrative archive:** the Persepolis Fortification tablets preserve
  traveler provisions, authorizations, road-station vouchers, and day-journey
  intervals. The surviving archive is regional, centered on Persis and Elam,
  and is not an empire-wide postal census.

## Prohibited inferences

No kilometre conversion, exact path, equal station spacing, traffic volume,
construction chronology, courier duration, army-march rate, postal census,
imperial-speed score, or complete imperial network is derived.

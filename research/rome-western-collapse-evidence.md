# Rome insight 04 — western collapse evidence audit

## Working claim

The western court did not disappear because of one decadent habit, one battle,
or one year. It lost taxable territory while repeatedly spending Roman resources
in civil wars and failed recoveries. The Vandal seizure of the richest African
provinces in 439 was an accelerant because it cut the revenue that could sustain
a permanent army; the Roman state continued in the East.

## What the visual can show

The event timeline is a chronology, not a quantitative causal score. It separates
political division, civil war, external pressure, fiscal workarounds, territorial
loss, surviving fiscal evidence, and failed recovery. Each event is keyed to Peter
Heather's synthesis and, where applicable, Bret Devereaux's public discussion.
Events are intentionally sparse: the chart does not pretend that twelve markers
contain every relevant cause.

The Africa comparison reproduces Peter Heather's published fiscal-equivalent
calculation. Heather uses Hugh Elton's estimated annual maintenance costs of six
solidi for a field infantryman and 10.5 solidi for a cavalryman. Heather estimates:

- damage to or loss of Numidia and the Mauretanias: revenue equivalent to about
  18,000 infantry or 10,000 cavalry;
- loss of Proconsularis and Byzacena: about 40,000 infantry or more than 20,000
  cavalry.

Adding the components yields 58,000 infantry-equivalent or a lower bound of
30,000 cavalry-equivalent. These are model outputs, not an observed dismissal of
58,000 soldiers, not a fifth-century western army census, and not a claim that
every lost solidus would otherwise have been spent on troops.

## The primary-law anchor

Valentinian III's Novel XIII, issued 21 June 445, records relief for Numidia and
Mauretania Sitifensis after public disaster and attacks. It retracts seven parts
and requires the eighth part of the earlier assessment. It also preserves named
amounts for the remaining obligation. The visual uses only the defensible ratio:
one-eighth remaining. It does not treat the named solidi, annonae, and capita as
interchangeable units or add them into a fictional total.

The one-eighth figure applies only to the named remaining provinces. It is not a
measurement of all African output, all western revenue, or an empire-wide tax
decline.

## What is deliberately excluded

- No continuous western revenue or army series: the surviving evidence cannot
  support one.
- No percentage of total western revenue lost in 439: published descriptions of
  Africa as exceptionally important do not produce a secure denominator.
- No exact 468 expedition cost: modern estimates vary and depend on converting
  unlike monetary evidence.
- No monocausal score for invasion, taxation, Christianity, climate, slavery, or
  political competence.
- No claim that 476 ended Rome. It ended the sequence of western emperors; the
  eastern Roman state continued.

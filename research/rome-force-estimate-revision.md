# Rome force-estimate revision

Date: 2026-08-29

Scope: every Roman point in `public/data/roman-military-capacity.csv`, the lines
drawn between those points, and the treatment of the eastern and western armies
after 395 CE.

## Result

The chart now contains seven Roman anchors rather than twenty-three. It does not
draw a single millennium-long Roman line. Points connect only inside four short
segments whose polity, scope, and estimation family are comparable:

1. Republican wartime mobilization, 225–212 BCE.
2. Principate regular establishment estimates, 24–200 CE.
3. Whole-empire late Roman establishment estimates, c. 300–337 CE.
4. One eastern Notitia-based paper-establishment model, plotted at c. 440.

Blank intervals are evidence gaps. They are not interpreted as zero soldiers,
steady strength, or linear change.

## Removed Roman points

The revision removes broad project reconstructions at 500, 400, 300, 250, 200,
167, and 100 BCE; the converted civil-war peak at 49 BCE; the Augustan, Flavian,
Trajanic, Diocletianic, and mid-fourth-century filler points; and every combined
eastern-plus-western value after 395. Their notes were candid, but the chart's
continuous line made those estimates look more homogeneous and better observed
than they were.

The earlier low/high columns and visible “model range” readout are also removed.
Published disagreements remain in notes and evidence classes rather than being
turned into project-selected interval endpoints.

## The Notitia date problem

The *Notitia Dignitatum* lists offices, commands, and units, not reliable unit
headcounts. Its eastern and western sections also do not preserve one clean,
uncontested snapshot.

- Treadgold's model counts the eastern register as about 303,000 soldiers:
  3,500 guards, 104,000 field troops, and 195,500 frontier troops, excluding
  fleets. He treats the structure as c. 395.
- Kaldellis and Kruse (2023) argue from narrative, legal, papyrological, and
  epigraphic evidence that the eastern five-army system emerged only in the
  440s. Their account describes the early fifth-century east as
  under-militarized before gradual remilitarization in the 420s and reform in
  the 440s.
- Rance's scholarly review accepts the need for reassessment but emphasizes
  that the redating rests on a small and ambiguous command dataset and creates
  unresolved problems for the rest of the document.

The chart therefore plots the eastern model at 440 and labels its display date
“c. 395 or c. 440s.” This is a visualization of a published model whose date is
disputed, not a claim that 303,000 soldiers were counted in 440.

No western fifth-century total is plotted. The current audit found descriptions
of western units and losses, but not a defensible, definition-compatible
establishment point that could be placed beside Treadgold's eastern model
without a new project calculation. Omitting it is more informative than
continuing the former combined decline line through 425, 476, and 500.

## Sources added

- Warren Treadgold, *Byzantium and Its Army, 284–1081* (Stanford University
  Press, 1995), especially pp. 49–59.
- *Notitia Dignitatum*, the official late Roman register of civil and military
  offices and named units.
- Anthony Kaldellis and Marion Kruse, *The Field Armies of the East Roman
  Empire, 361–630* (Cambridge University Press, 2023), especially chapters 2–3
  and appendix 4.
- Philip Rance, review of Kaldellis and Kruse, *Bryn Mawr Classical Review*
  2024.11.28.

The source registry contains public catalog, primary-text, publisher, and review
links. None of these sources supports annual interpolation.

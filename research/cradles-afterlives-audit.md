# Cradles insight 05 — historical afterlives evidence audit

## Editorial claim

The end of a capital, a settlement form, or a regional state is not one shared
historical event. The public comparison therefore follows six bounded pathways
from a prior system through an observable transition to what is visible after it.
It does not compute a collapse, resilience, continuity, or population-loss score.

## Comparison rule

Crawford et al. (2023) define settlement persistence as the duration of continuous
occupation at a well-defined place. That is a useful measurable concept, but it is
not interchangeable with persistence of a population, political order, archive,
institution, language, or identity. The visualization keeps those units separate.

## Included cases

- **Mesopotamia — deliberate dispersal.** Glatz et al. radiocarbon-bound four
  institutional phases at Shakhi Kora followed by deliberate dismantling and
  dispersal. This is an Upper Diyala case and is not generalized to all
  Mesopotamia; the residents' destinations and a single cause are unknown.
- **Egypt — political relocation.** Charloux et al. provide a securely dated
  Karnak sequence around 2200–2000 BCE and connect the retreating Nile bank,
  Eleventh Dynasty power, and the rise of Thebes. This supports a new political
  center, not uninterrupted national government across the First Intermediate
  Period.
- **Indus — deurbanization and regional shift.** Petrie et al., Madella and
  Fuller, and Dixit et al. support regionally variable contraction and an eastern
  shift after Mature urbanism. Climate is associated with the transformation but
  is not treated as a sole cause; urban contraction is not a disappearance count.
- **Northern China — capital destruction.** He Nu describes the destruction of
  Taosi's palace enclosure, palaces, enceinte, altar, and royal tombs and argues
  for conquest and political retaliation. The site continued but ceased to be a
  capital. The interpretation does not identify a conqueror, historical dynasty,
  or direct line of succession to Erlitou.
- **Mesoamerica — regional fragmentation.** Kowalewski et al.'s 1,119-date
  Oaxaca database and selected architectural sequences show Monte Albán reaching
  maximum size and then fragmenting while Lambityeco, El Palmillo, and
  Macuilxochitl changed unevenly between about 500 and 1000 CE. These selected
  sequences are not a valley-wide population series.
- **Andes — hypothesized reorganization.** Sandweiss et al. argue that an early
  Supe adaptation was abandoned and hypothesize a sequence of earthquake,
  El Niño flooding, beach-ridge formation, and dune incursion. The sequence is
  retained as a testable hypothesis, not a proven monocause or a migration map.

## Exclusions

- No shared date axis: the cases range across millennia and unlike phases.
- No population totals or losses: none of the six sources supplies comparable
  demographic observations.
- No successor arrows where descent or institutional inheritance is unproven.
- No claim that unreadable or absent records mean absent administration.
- No climate-collapse series: association, mechanism, and causation remain
  source-specific.

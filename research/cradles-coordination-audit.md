# Cradles insight 04 — coordination-route audit

## Question

How did early urban regions turn food, materials, exchange, and labor into
coordinated action without assuming that surplus automatically created a state?

## Decision

The public dataset contains four source-keyed routes for each of six regional
cases. A route is a short causal vocabulary—input, coordinator, and visible
outcome—not a quantity or a claim that one institution controlled the whole
economy. The twenty-four rows remain archaeological or textual observations
with unlike evidence classes. They are never counted as prevalence, ranked, or
combined into a capacity score.

The interface reads one regional portfolio at a time. This avoids repeating the
ecology insight's six-by-five matrix: ecology asks what physical combinations
sustained settlement; this insight asks what social devices made resources and
labor usable beyond an isolated household.

## Regional source map

- **Mesopotamia:** ENGLUND2011 and ORACC_DCCLT anchor direct evidence for
  institutional accounting. GLATZ_ET_AL2025 supplies repeated supra-household
  kitchens before deliberate dispersal. POURNELLE_ALGAZE2014 and
  ALIVERNINI_GRECO_WIDELL2026 keep herds, fish, orchards, and grain visible.
  CHAZAN_LEHNER1990 makes the mass-produced bowl route explicitly contested.
- **Egypt:** MOELLER2015 documents brewery installations beyond household scale,
  specialized craft zones, production linked to an elite cemetery, and a
  late-Predynastic exchange network. It also states that workers and production
  cannot be estimated precisely; no tonnage or labor force is invented here.
- **Indus:** GREEN_ALAM_PETRIE2026 brings together shared weights, seals,
  accessible non-residential structures, streets, drainage, and massive
  foundations. GREEN2020 supplies the collective-governance interpretation and
  the absence of recovered palaces, royal tombs, and a ruling class. Shared
  protocols and public goods are evidence; a deciphered constitution is not.
- **Northern China:** HE2018 describes Taosi's segregated storage and craft zones
  while clearly marking authority, tax, guard, and bureaucratic control as
  interpretations. XU2018 anchors the Erlitou capital and workshop model without
  settling its dynastic identity or providing comparable labor and output totals.
- **Mesoamerica:** NICHOLAS_FEINMAN2022 supplies household intensification,
  marketplace buffering, and small distributed water works in the Valley of
  Oaxaca. INOMATA_ET_AL2020 supplies the dated Aguada Fénix platform and its
  communal-work interpretation before later royal courts.
- **Andes:** SHADY_ET_AL2001 dates Caral's irrigation and monuments;
  SANDWEISS_ET_AL2009 documents the cotton–net-fishing and valley-production
  complement; HAAS_ET_AL2013 adds multi-proxy maize evidence;
  GONZALEZ_GARCIA_ET_AL2021 supplies the river, coast, and monument setting.

## Claims deliberately excluded

- No row estimates surplus, tax take, storage volume, labor days, coercion, or
  the share of production controlled by an institution.
- The number of published routes is not a measure of institutional diversity or
  state capacity. Every region has four rows because the interface needs an
  inspectable portfolio, not because history supplied equal evidence.
- Standard weights do not identify an enforcing ruler; public goods do not prove
  political equality; monuments do not reveal voluntary or forced labor.
- Irrigation does not prove hydraulic government. Exchange and complementarity
  do not prove a market, central redistribution, or recorded transaction volume.
- Bevel-rim bowls do not establish grain wages, forced labor, slavery, or one use.
- Later or mixed-genre Mesopotamian texts are not fourth-millennium economic totals.

## Review gate

Validation requires exactly twenty-four unique region-route rows and exactly four
per region. Every row needs a registered source, an evidence status, and a
substantive limit. Guard clauses preserve the Mesopotamian bowl ambiguity, the
Egyptian brewery's missing production totals, the Indus ruler gap, Taosi's
interpretive tax/storage model, Aguada Fénix labor uncertainty, and the absence
of centralized redistribution in the Andean complementarity evidence. Any score,
rank, weight, index, prevalence count, or common-scale numeric field is rejected.

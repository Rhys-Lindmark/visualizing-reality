# Qin/Han insight 02 audit — standards made interfaces, not sameness

## Claim under test

Qin standardization made distant administration more mutually legible by circulating certified objects, official scripts, document dimensions, and record fields. It did not instantaneously make every object, hand, or local practice identical. Surviving evidence is strongest when a formal norm can be compared directly with an excavated local trace.

## Source map

- **Thies Staack, “The pragmatics of standardization” (2023, CC BY):** the central comparison between Qin ordinances and excavated Liye documents. Its bounded samples preserve twenty-five upward reports and twenty-two grain-disbursal tallies, including exact compliance and deviation counts.
- **Ondřej Škrabal, “A Scoop of Filial Piety?” (2023):** the object biography of a 980 ml Qin palace scoop used before unification, certified with the forty-character edict in 221 BCE, then re-inscribed with the sixty-character Second Emperor supplement in 209 BCE. It demonstrates circulation through certification, templates, local engraving, and reuse.
- **National Museum of China, Qin First and Second Emperor double-edict plate:** museum object record confirming the 221 and 209 BCE inscriptions and their attachment to standard measures or public promulgation.
- **Imre Galambos, “The Myth of the Qin Unification of Writing in Han Sources” (2004):** epigraphic critique of a sudden, comprehensive 221 BCE script change. It treats the later story as politically shaped and the observed process as gradual.

## Design decision

The visual asks how closely a standard matched practice. Two qualitative rows compare certification and official script. Four quantitative rows compare twenty-five document tablets or twenty-two grain tallies. The bars use only like-for-like counts within each published sample; they are not combined into a standardization score.

## Claims deliberately excluded

- One edict instantly replaced every regional measure or writing practice.
- “Unified writing” means a unified spoken language.
- A 980 ml excavated scoop defines one exact empire-wide modern conversion.
- The Liye sample represents every Qin county.
- Every formal deviation was detected or punished.
- Document compliance measures political loyalty, administrative capacity, literacy, or bureaucratic efficiency.
- Six unlike evidence windows can be averaged into one standardization percentage.

## Data contract

`qin-standardization-practice.csv` contains six evidence windows. Each preserves the channel, date, formal norm or later claim, observed practice, sample label, source keys, interpretation, and inference limit. Four rows retain published bounded counts. Empty numeric cells are intentional for qualitative evidence; there are no fields for an empire-wide compliance rate, conversion table, literacy, loyalty, efficiency, or state-capacity score.

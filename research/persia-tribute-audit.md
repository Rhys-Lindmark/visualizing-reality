# Persia insight 03 evidence audit — obligation was a portfolio

## Published claim

The Achaemenid Empire did not impose one comparable tax rate. Surviving evidence instead exposes portfolios of silver, goods, provisions, people, service, gifts, and status relationships. Herodotus's district schedule is useful, but it is a Greek literary accounting model rather than a surviving imperial budget.

## Source hierarchy

1. Herodotus 3.89–97 supplies the twenty-district schedule, the Cilician and Egyptian mixed obligations, and three frontier gift calendars.
2. Cuneiform documentary syntheses by Dandamayev and Kleber supply the counterweight: Persian and Babylonian records preserve taxes and obligations in silver, barley, flour, livestock, beer, provisions, and service.
3. The Encyclopaedia Iranica Apadana entry documents the original program of twenty-three delegations and cautions against reading the relief as a literal annual ledger or proof of one ceremony.
4. The Iranica gift-giving synthesis keeps gift, tribute, obligation, and status connected without reducing them to a voluntary/compulsory binary.

## The Herodotean schedule

The visual reproduces all twenty reported districts in order. Nineteen silver assessments sum to 7,740 Babylonian talents. India is kept outside that bar scale because Herodotus reports 360 Euboic talents of gold dust. Herodotus's own later conversions remain his arithmetic; they are not converted into modern money, kilograms, or verified receipts.

One public-domain transcription of 3.90 prints 300 talents for the first district. The Perseus text, Encyclopaedia Iranica's summary, and the internal 7,740-talent silver arithmetic support 400. The dataset therefore uses 400 and records the discrepancy here rather than silently harmonizing it.

## Why districts are not satrapies

Dandamayev notes that Herodotus's *nomoi* only partially correspond to the country lists in Persian inscriptions. The chart labels them reported districts and does not draw precise borders or reuse the satrapal cases as if the two systems were identical.

## Why the Apadana is not a receipt book

The relief program represents differentiated delegations approaching the king with characteristic goods and animals. It is strong evidence for an ideology of ordered diversity and royal reception. It is not a dated record of twenty-three annual deliveries, a measurable tax base, or proof that one specific ceremony generated the scene.

## Deliberate exclusions

- no modern currency, metal-weight conversion, purchasing power, per-capita burden, tax rate, revenue share, or effective incidence;
- no ranking of districts by wealth, loyalty, importance, or exploitation;
- no assumption that tribute districts equal satrapies;
- no conversion of horses, grain, gold dust, people, service, and gifts into silver;
- no assumption that gifts were simply voluntary or that every obligation was collected exactly as Herodotus reports;
- no empire-wide total assembled from unlike documentary, literary, and monumental evidence.

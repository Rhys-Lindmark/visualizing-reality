# Persia insight 05 evidence audit — institutional afterlives

## Published claim

The Achaemenid dynasty ended, but parts of its imperial toolkit remained usable. Provincial offices could be retained and re-staffed, military interfaces could be experimented with and abandoned, chancellery writing could branch into new scripts, local Persian dynasts could recombine old forms, and royal titles could be revived after a gap. These are different historical relationships, not one continuity score.

## Five pathways

1. **Province as a reusable unit.** Alexander preserved most eastern territorial structures while changing personnel; the Babylon partition continued to allocate satrapies, and Seleucid Upper Satrapies adapted some former imperial geography. Boundaries, staff, and fiscal practice did not remain fixed.
2. **Army as a coalition interface.** Alexander placed Iranian commanders and formations inside Macedonian-named structures late in his reign. Opis shows both the experiment and its political resistance. No shared mixed army can be followed through the successor settlement.
3. **Chancery writing became many scripts.** Provincial Aramaic traditions diverged into several Iranian scripts. The Persis coin sequence makes one paleographic bridge visible, but no empire-wide archive survives across the whole period.
4. **Local Persian rule recombined old forms.** Frataraka dynasts probably began as Seleucid representatives, used Aramaic legends and Achaemenid-looking imagery, and later operated beneath Arsacid power. Their status, title, and territorial reach changed.
5. **King of Kings was revived, not inherited continuously.** The Achaemenid title is not plotted as an unbroken Macedonian or Seleucid line. Arsacid kings revived it with new genealogy and imagery; Sasanians redesigned it as “King of Kings of Iran and non-Iran.”

## Source hierarchy

- Primary narrative: Diodorus 18.3 for the Babylon partition; Arrian 7.6–11 for the Opis reorganization and reaction.
- Royal and documentary evidence: Achaemenid inscriptions and Aramaic records already registered elsewhere in the project.
- Epigraphy and numismatics: Humbach on the development of Iranian scripts; Wiesehöfer on the frataraka; Dąbrowa on Arsacid titulature; Daryaee and Shahbazi on Sasanian titulature.
- Political synthesis: Jacobs on satrapal continuity and Kosmin on Seleucid imperial structure.

## Deliberate exclusions

- No institutional-continuity, influence, centralization, Persianization, or state-capacity score.
- No continuous territorial polygon from the Achaemenids to later Iranian dynasties.
- No assumption that shared titles imply unchanged government.
- No claim that script descent equals bureaucratic continuity.
- No claim that Alexander's late army experiment became a stable successor institution.
- No visual duration is interpreted as institutional strength or importance.

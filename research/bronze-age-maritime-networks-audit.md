# Bronze Age maritime networks — evidence audit

## Claim under test

Late Bronze Age maritime connectivity crossed political worlds through overlapping elite voyages, freelance metal ventures, coasting cargoes, and harbor communities. It should not be represented as one centrally owned market or one reconstructed route network.

## Why these five windows

The cases are deliberately unlike. Three wrecks preserve bounded cargo events; two harbor sites accumulate material across many contexts and years. Their incompatibility is the argument: several carriers and institutions made the same sea connected.

| Window | What survives | Bounded anchor | What is not inferred |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Uluburun | Bulk metal, containers, elite goods, weights, personal effects, hull and passengers | approximately 10 tonnes copper plus 1 tonne tin; at least 149 Canaanite jars | representative cargo, exact itinerary, destination, one owner, or complete palace control |
| Cape Gelidonya | Ingots, scrap bronze, weights and metalworking tools | more than 750 documented copper-ingot fragments, at least one-third of the shipment | 750 complete ingots, full cargo total, named occupation, or secure ship/crew identity |
| Point Iria | Mixed Cypriot, Cretan and mainland Mycenaean transport and serving pottery | three ceramic production traditions | surviving organic contents, crew ethnicity, or observed Cyprus–Crete–Argolid itinerary |
| Kommos | Targeted archaeometric sample plus excavated imported containers and ingot fragments | 69 analyzed fragments; roughly 80 percent support the proposed Cypriot/Mycenaean classifications inside that sample | total imports, import share, annual ships, or direct voyages |
| Hala Sultan Tekke | Multi-period imports, urban production and harbor settlement | published minimum city extent of 25 hectares | simultaneous population, annual imports, export volume, market share, or a typical year |

## Source decisions

- **INA Uluburun project** is the excavation institution's public synthesis. It anchors cargo diversity, at least 149 Canaanite jars, the Syro-Canaanite crew interpretation, Greek-mainland passengers and the Lebanese-cedar hull. Hauptmann et al. retain the metal masses; Manning et al. retain the late-fourteenth-century time capsule.
- **INA Cape Gelidonya project and ingot update** anchor the scrap, tools, tinker interpretation, uncertain vessel identity and the later count of more than 750 documented fragments. The number is never presented as complete ingots or the full shipment.
- **Point Iria excavation proceedings** anchor the mixed Cypriot, Cretan and mainland Mycenaean ceramic cargo. The proposed voyage remains an interpretation and is not drawn.
- **Tomlinson, Rutter and Hoffmann** anchor Kommos's 69-fragment neutron-activation sample and roughly 80-percent result. The official Kommos excavation guide adds Canaanite and Egyptian containers while explicitly allowing indirect arrival.
- **Fischer and the Austrian Archaeological Institute project** anchor Hala Sultan Tekke's minimum extent, scientific import identifications, multiple eastern Mediterranean connections, Sardinian reach and local production. The cases span centuries and are not synchronized.
- **Kristiansen and Suchowska-Ducke** provide the comparative framing that Uluburun, Cape Gelidonya and Point Iria represent different modes of exchange. Their synthesis is used as interpretation, not as a substitute for the excavation records.

## Map semantics

The nineteen dashed connections join a find context to a material, analytical, or typological region named by a source. They are not sailing routes. They do not encode distance traveled, direction, frequency, quantity, intermediaries, political permission, ownership, or market integration.

Coordinates are approximate display locations for archaeological sites and broad provenance regions. Political settings appear as prose because Late Bronze Age sovereignty, territorial extent and the relation between material culture and political identity are not precise enough for these cases to support a common border layer.

## Anti-fabrication rules

The public data and validator reject fields or claims that would imply:

- annual traffic, import or export volume;
- market share, port rank or connectivity score;
- direct voyages or a complete itinerary;
- crew ethnicity, owner, departure or destination;
- complete political control or a single trade institution;
- a representative cargo or typical year;
- missing evidence as zero.

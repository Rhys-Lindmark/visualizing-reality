# Bronze Age palace circuits — evidence audit

## Claim under test

Bronze Age palaces could make selected flows of food, animals, metal, labor, and information visible, storable, or steerable. “Palace economy” should not be expanded into a claim that a palace controlled the entire economy around it.

## Why these five cases

The comparison is designed around unlike but unusually legible institutional apertures. It is not a representative sample and does not rank centralization.

| Case | Selected circuit | Anchor retained | Inference blocked |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mari | Store accounts, rations, deliveries, taxes, and correspondence inside a palace archive | 15,000 Amorite-period texts across about half a century | Annual transaction count, total workforce, or whole economy |
| Hattusha | Mobilized hulled wheat and barley preserved in one underground silo | One intact sixteenth-century BCE storage episode | Empire-wide capacity, annual tax series, or state direction of cultivation |
| Knossos | Linear B flock records connected sheep management to wool production | About 66,000 sheep in roughly 600 recorded flocks | Complete livestock census or annual cloth output |
| Pylos | Bronze weighed out from administrative holdings to dispersed smiths | Individual allotments of 1.5–12 kg | Finished objects, all smiths, annual output, or palace share of metalworking |
| Ugarit | Royal and household archives linked official and commercial roles | About 600 tablets/fragments in Urtenu’s house versus 1,040 in the royal palace archive | Private-trade volume or a clean modern public/private boundary |

## Evidence decisions

### Mari

The French Ministry of Culture excavation guide is used for the current corpus framing and its division between shakkanakku and Amorite documentation. Dossin’s 1939 preliminary publication anchors the palace-room and economic-archive context but is not treated as the current corpus total. The selected row combines writing and storage administration because the modern guide identifies personnel rations, animal and textile deliveries, “king’s meals” grain-store accounts, and taxes.

### Hattusha

Diffey and colleagues combine direct archaeobotany, crop stable isotopes, and functional weed ecology. Their evidence supports state mobilization of grain raised under varied extensive regimes. It does not support a smooth tax curve or the claim that the palace dictated field practice. The chart therefore uses “one intact silo episode” instead of a capacity or tonnage estimate.

### Knossos

Killen establishes the administrative relationship between the Knossos sheep archive and wool production. Halstead’s demographic critique is essential: the male-heavy census cannot reproduce itself biologically and apparent deficits encode administrative relationships. The chart retains the bounded tally while refusing to call it all Cretan livestock.

### Pylos

Nakassis’s prosopographic analysis gives the 1.5–12 kg allotment range and argues against treating every smith as a homogeneous full-time palace worker. Judson establishes the short-lived material recording process and the final destruction horizon of most surviving Pylian texts. Nakassis, Parkinson, and Galaty provide the larger warning that one “redistributive economy” label hides the institutional question.

### Ugarit

Malbran-Labat and Roche document the House of Urtenu archive and its scale relative to the palace archive. Gilibert integrates household archives and urban archaeology, locating a probable market away from centrally administered deliveries and finding substantial business outside direct palace control. The chart therefore calls the system distributed, but does not recast Urtenu as a modern private company.

## Excluded operations

- No sum of texts, sheep, kilograms, or archaeological episodes.
- No palace-capacity, centralization, redistribution, bureaucracy, or state-strength score.
- No annual interpolation from destruction-horizon tablets or one burned silo.
- No conversion of archive size into transaction volume, population, literacy, or economic share.
- No assumption that “outside the palace” means free market, political independence, or absence of hierarchy.

## Product contract

Every public row contains an input, palace aperture, transformation, visible result, outside-palace observation, evidence channels, a bounded anchor, source keys, and an inference limit. The client uses an immutable snapshot and fails closed if the five-case schema changes. Automated validation guards all five anchor contexts, rejects shared-scale fields, and requires byte-for-byte equality between canonical and client data.

# Cradles insight 02 — ecology comparison audit

## Question

Can six early urban regions be compared across water, rainfall, crops, transport,
and settlement form without turning unlike evidence into a hydraulic-state score?

## Decision

The public dataset is a 6 × 5 matrix of qualitative, source-keyed observations.
Each cell keeps its own place, chronology, evidence status, interpretation, and
limit. The interface permits reading down one region or across one dimension.
It computes no numeric intensity, productivity, aridity, legibility, density, or
state-causation index.

“Settlement form” replaces a numeric settlement-density field. Regional survey
coverage, preservation, phase length, and published population models are too
unlike to support a common density measure. The visual describes concentration,
distribution, and landscape position while explaining why those descriptions
cannot be ranked.

## Regional source map

- **Mesopotamia:** ALTAWEEL_ET_AL2019 bounds an environmental sequence near the
  Uruk–Larsa region; POURNELLE_ALGAZE2014 supplies the deltaic grain–herd–marsh
  synthesis; JOTHERI_ET_AL2025 prevents the preserved canal palimpsest from being
  back-projected as one simultaneous Uruk network; ALIVERNINI_GRECO_WIDELL2026
  provides explicitly later textual evidence for resource variety.
- **Egypt:** MOELLER2015 is used as one settlement-archaeology synthesis for the
  Nile floodplain, riverine connection, cereal production, and Hierakonpolis.
  Alluvial burial and the visible desert-edge footprint are kept as limits.
- **Indus:** PETRIE_ET_AL2017 documents diverse ecologies at the overlap of winter
  and summer rainfall in northwest India. MADELLA_FULLER2006 blocks a simple
  climate-collapse story. GREEN_PETRIE2018 supports the polycentric settlement
  frame. None is generalized into one empire-wide crop or water system.
- **Northern China:** LIU_HUNT_JONES2009 places early millet farming in dry,
  well-drained foothills as well as river regions. ZHUANG2024 supplies bounded
  contrasts between Taosi's loess tableland/land routes and Erlitou's terrace at
  a river confluence. Two sites are not treated as all northern China.
- **Mesoamerica:** NICHOLAS_FEINMAN2022 supplies one internally coherent Oaxaca
  comparison: unreliable rainfall, scarce permanent water at Monte Albán,
  maize-centered household production, small-scale water works, marketplace
  buffering, and simultaneous urban and rural growth. No top-down hydraulic
  command is inferred.
- **Andes:** SHADY_ET_AL2001 anchors Caral's dated urban and irrigation evidence;
  GONZALEZ_GARCIA_ET_AL2021 links the Supe River, seasonal floods, agriculture,
  and the marine setting; SANDWEISS_ET_AL2009 documents fishing, irrigated cotton
  and orchards while labeling its disaster sequence as a hypothesis;
  HAAS_ET_AL2013 adds multi-proxy evidence for substantial maize use and preserves
  the earlier sampling debate.

## Claims deliberately excluded

- No region receives a hydraulic, environmental-efficiency, or civilization score.
- “Riverine” is not equated with centralized irrigation management.
- Climate is not presented as a sufficient cause of state formation or collapse.
- Crop observations are not converted into calorie or surplus shares.
- Transport observations are not converted into traffic volumes.
- Settlement descriptions are not converted into a shared density measure.
- Later Mesopotamian texts are not treated as direct fourth-millennium production totals.

## Review gate

The dataset must contain exactly thirty unique region–dimension cells, five for
each of six regions. Every cell requires registered source keys and a non-empty
limit. Validation rejects numeric score/rank fields and language that claims one
ecology proves centralized hydraulic control or directly caused state formation.

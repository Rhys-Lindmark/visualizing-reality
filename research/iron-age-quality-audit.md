# Iron Age insight 03 — metallography and the limits of “better”

## Bounded claim

Iron quality was made through a sequence of smelting and working choices. In one controlled four-run study, hardness, carbon distribution, sulfur and phosphorus, slag inclusions, and forgeability did not move together. The hardest mean bloom was brittle and difficult to forge. This rejects a one-number material ranking without claiming that hardness is irrelevant.

## Evidence table

Stepanov et al. (2022), Table 5, reports paired bloom and bar averages for four experiments. The public rows preserve phosphorus, sulfur, Vickers hardness, standard deviation, and hardness sample count. Microstructure and forgeability statements come from the paper's metallographic discussion and direct forging observations.

- FEXP-1: bloom 103 HV, bar 142 HV; localized carburization in the bloom and a small mild-steel patch in the bar; better workability.
- FEXP-100: bloom 89 HV, bar 114 HV; ferritic despite a protocol intended to promote a steely bloom; brittle.
- FEXP-5: bloom 84 HV, bar 102 HV; ferritic and comparatively workable.
- FEXP-6: bloom 139 HV, bar 113 HV; higher bloom hardness linked to phosphorus, with sulfur-rich inclusions, cracking, and difficult forging.

All reported hardness values are sample means, not whole-object properties. Bars were made from quarters of blooms and were nonuniform, so metal loss cannot be compared consistently.

## Prohibited inferences

- no iron-versus-bronze superiority score;
- no conversion of Vickers hardness into toughness, weapon lethality, service life, or battlefield outcome;
- no claim that one experimental run is a named ancient recipe;
- no ranking of regions, civilizations, smelters, or technologies;
- no uniform-steel label for a heterogeneous bloom;
- no projection from four modern runs to average ancient iron.

## Visual contract

Pair bloom and bar hardness for each run, expose sample size and spread, and make chemistry, microstructure, and forgeability inspectable. The interface must foreground FEXP-6 as the counterexample: the hardest mean bloom was not the easiest material to turn into a useful bar.
